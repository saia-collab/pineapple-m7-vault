## H2 — Why Frisco roofs take hail harder than most
Frisco sits in the heart of North Texas hail alley. Storms roll off the plains and hit Collin and Denton County with stones that bruise shingles without breaking them. That bruising fractures the mat beneath the granules — the layer that actually keeps water out. From your driveway, the roof looks perfect. Under a camera at six feet, it doesn't.

That gap between *looks fine* and *is fine* is where homeowners lose claims.

## H2 — What hail actually does to a shingle (and why it's easy to dismiss)
- **Granule loss** — the sandpaper surface sheds, exposing the mat to UV.
- **Mat fracture** — a soft bruise you can feel but rarely see from the ground.
- **Seal strip failure** — wind lifts shingles that hail already loosened.
- **Collateral evidence** — dents on gutters, downspouts, vents, and A/C fins that corroborate the storm date.

That last one matters. Adjusters look for a *consistent story* across the whole property. A photo of one shingle is an opinion. A documented pattern is evidence.

### H3 — The claim-window problem nobody explains
Texas policies generally require you to report damage promptly, and every storm that passes makes it harder to prove *which* storm caused *what*. Wait long enough and a legitimate claim starts to look like deferred maintenance. **Documentation is time-sensitive — that's the honest urgency.**
