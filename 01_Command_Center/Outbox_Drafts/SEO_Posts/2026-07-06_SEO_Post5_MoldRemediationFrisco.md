## Why mold remediation in Frisco TX is a containment problem

Three things make a Frisco mold project fail when the contractor is "spraying and replacing":

- **Cross-contamination.** A non-contained removal spreads mold spores into adjacent rooms and into the HVAC return. The visible problem is solved; the hidden problem grows. Six months later, the project is back.
- **Source confusion.** The visible mold is rarely the only mold. A surface remediation that does not open the wall cavity, inspect the insulation, and check the HVAC misses the substrate. The project "looks done" and is not.
- **No post-remediation verification.** A project that does not end with an independent air sample is a project the homeowner's next buyer, lender, or carrier will question. The IICRC S520 standard mandates independent verification for a reason.

Pineapple Restorations is built around containment. The affected area is sealed with 6-mil polyethylene. Negative-pressure machines run continuously during the removal. HEPA air filtration scrubs the contained volume. The HVAC is isolated or filtered. The result is a project that is verifiable, not just visible.

## The 7 stages of Pineapple Restorations mold remediation

### Stage 1 — 60-minute on-site assessment

The Pineapple emergency line is **972-928-0788**. The first hour is a loss-documentation visit: photograph the visible growth, identify the moisture source, measure the affected square footage, sample the spore count if it has not been sampled, and produce a written scope. The scope is the artifact the carrier's desk expects.

### Stage 2 — Independent air sampling (when needed)

Pineapple coordinates with an independent indoor air quality (IAQ) consultant for pre-remediation spore counts. The sample establishes the baseline and identifies the species. Stachybotrys, Aspergillus, Penicillium, Cladosporium — each species has a different removal implication and a different health-screening threshold.

### Stage 3 — Source removal and containment

The affected area is sealed with 6-mil polyethylene from floor to ceiling. Negative-pressure machines run continuously during the removal. HEPA air filtration runs on the contained volume. The HVAC system is isolated, filtered, or shut down depending on the project's contamination depth.

### Stage 4 — Material removal to the substrate

Porous materials (drywall, insulation, carpet, padding, ceiling tile) colonized by mold are removed to the substrate. Semi-porous materials (wood framing, subfloor) are HEPA-vacuumed and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial. Non-porous materials (metal, glass, hard plastic) are HEPA-vacuumed, wet-wiped, and treated.

### Stage 5 — Antimicrobial application and HEPA air filtration

Pineapple applies an EPA-registered antimicrobial to all exposed substrate and cavity surfaces. HEPA air filtration continues throughout the containment phase. Air exchanges in the contained volume are tracked and logged.

### Stage 6 — Post-remediation verification

The Pineapple crew does not declare the project complete. An independent IAQ consultant performs a post-remediation air sample. The clearance sample is compared to the pre-remediation baseline and to an outdoor control sample. The clearance certificate is the artifact the homeowner's next buyer, lender, or carrier will ask for.

### Stage 7 — Rebuild and Full Restoration Coverage Evaluation

When the clearance certificate lands, Pineapple Restorations prepares a **Full Restoration Coverage Evaluation** — a value-engineered line-item scope that matches the carrier's scope, adds any code-required upgrades, and protects your family's investment through the rebuild phase. ## What the IICRC S520 standard means on your Frisco loss

The **Institute of Inspection Cleaning and Restoration Certification (IICRC)** publishes **S520 — Standard for Professional Mold Remediation**. It is the only ANSI-accredited standard for the trade. A non-certified "mold company" in Frisco will quote the visible work and skip the rest. A Pineapple Restorations crew trained to S520 will:

- **Class the project by contamination level** — Level 1 (small, isolated), Level 2 (mid-sized HVAC or cavity involvement), Level 3 (whole-room or whole-HVAC contamination), Level 4 (extensive, multiple rooms, contractor PPE required).
- **Set containment to the class** — 6-mil polyethylene, single-door entry, negative-pressure HEPA filtration. The class drives the equipment and the cost.
- **Coordinate independent post-remediation verification** — the clearance sample is the project's closing document.
- **Document in the language the adjuster expects** — psychrometric readings, antimicrobial product labels, spore count comparison, equipment logs.

The IICRC S520 standard is the difference between a verifiable remediation and a recurring problem.
