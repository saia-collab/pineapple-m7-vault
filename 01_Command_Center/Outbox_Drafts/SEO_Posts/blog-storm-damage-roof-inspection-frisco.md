North Texas storms don't ask permission. One hail event or high-wind night can leave your Frisco roof with damage you can't see from the ground — and by the time a leak shows up inside, the problem is usually bigger (and costlier) than it had to be.

Here's exactly what to do after a storm, how a professional roof inspection works, and how to keep your options open with insurance.

## First, stay off the roof
It's tempting to grab a ladder and look for yourself. Don't. Wet or hail-battered roofing is slick and unstable, and untrained eyes miss the damage that matters most — bruised shingles, lifted flashing, and compromised seals. A ground-level look plus a few photos of any obvious debris is all you need before calling a professional.

## What storm damage actually looks like
After a North Texas storm, a trained inspector checks for:

- **Hail bruising** — soft, dark spots where the granules are knocked off, exposing the mat underneath. Often invisible from the ground.
- **Wind-lifted or missing shingles** — especially along ridges and edges.
- **Damaged flashing** — around chimneys, vents and valleys, where leaks usually start.
- **Granules in the gutters** — a sign the shingle surface is wearing prematurely.
- **Interior clues** — ceiling stains, attic moisture, or daylight through the decking.

Any one of these can turn into an interior leak if it's left alone through the next storm.
