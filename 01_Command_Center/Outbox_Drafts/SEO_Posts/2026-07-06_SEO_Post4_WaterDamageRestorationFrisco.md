## Why water damage restoration in Frisco TX is a clock problem

Three things happen to wet building materials on a predictable timeline:

- **Hours 0–24:** Water wicks into drywall, padding, and subfloor. Warping begins. Paper-faced gypsum loses 50% of its structural capacity once saturated.
- **Hours 24–48:** Bacteria begin to colonize the wetted materials. Musty odor starts. The carpet is recoverable; the padding often is not.
- **Hours 48–72:** Mold spores germinate in the wet substrate. Once mold is established, the project adds containment, HEPA air filtration, antimicrobial treatment, and demolition of colonized material. The scope and the cost both grow.

The Pineapple Restorations playbook is built around that timeline. A 60-minute response, a documented emergency mitigation, and a 3–5 day structural dry-out keep the project on the **mitigation** track, not the **rebuild** track.

Before any water is extracted, the project lead photographs every affected area, every piece of contents, and every building material. This is the documentation package the carrier will use to scope the loss. Skipping this step costs the claim.

### Stage 3 — Water extraction (hours 1–4)

Pineapple brings truck-mount and portable extractors rated for residential and commercial losses. Extraction removes 90% of the water before drying begins. The faster the extraction, the shorter the dry-out.

### Stage 4 — Demolition of non-salvageable materials

Wet padding, wet insulation, wet drywall cut to the flood line, and wet baseboards come out in the first 24 hours. The project lead documents each removal for the carrier and the rebuild team. The demolition is a controlled tear-out, not a gutting.

Pineapple treats the affected substrate with an EPA-registered antimicrobial and sets up HEPA air filtration during demolition. This is the step that prevents the hour 48–72 mold colonization. It is also the step that most non-certified mitigation crews skip.

## What the IICRC standard means on your Frisco loss

The **Institute of Inspection Cleaning and Restoration Certification (IICRC)** publishes the standards for water damage restoration. **S500** is the standard for water damage; **S520** is the standard for mold remediation; **S700** is the standard for pack-out and contents.

A non-certified mitigation company in Frisco will "do their best" with whatever equipment they have. A Pineapple Restorations crew trained to the IICRC standard will:

- **Size the dehumidification to the cubic footage** of the affected area, not the truck inventory.
- **Set drying goals by material class** — drywall to 1% moisture content, hardwood to 6–8%, concrete to 2–3%.
- **Document the psychrometric chart** with specific grains-per-pound and temperature targets, not "warm and dry."
- **Communicate the carrier-grade scope** in the language the adjuster's desk expects.

The IICRC standard is the difference between a completed dry-out and a contested claim.
