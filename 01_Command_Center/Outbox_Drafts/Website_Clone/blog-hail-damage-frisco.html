Hail Damage in Frisco: What Homeowners Need to Do in the First 72 Hours

Hail hits Frisco and the rest of Collin County harder than almost anywhere in North Texas. Here's the exact playbook our IKO Certified crew runs on every storm call — what to photograph, what to skip, how to talk to your insurance company, and how to avoid the storm-chasing traps.

If you just heard hail on the roof in Frisco last night — take a breath. Most of the time, the roof held. But the only way to know for sure is to look at it the right way, in the right order, before anything else happens. The first 60 minutes: stay safe, start the clock

Before you climb a ladder, photograph a shingle, or call anyone — get the timing right. Insurance carriers care a lot about when the damage was reported, and small steps now save big arguments later.

Hour 1 — Do these in order

Confirm the hail event. Check local radar, news, or the National Weather Service Fort Worth office for an official hail report near your address. Screenshot the timestamp.
Walk the property from the ground. Look for fallen branches, damaged fence panels, dented gutters, and shattered A/C fins. Photograph everything from the ground before you touch the roof.
Open a claim file mentally. Even if you don't call your carrier for 48 hours, write down the date, time, and what you observed. Your future self will thank you.
Check cars in the driveway. Car damage on the hood, roof, or windshield is excellent corroborating evidence. Photograph VIN-visible damage with a timestamp.

Hours 1–24: what to photograph (and what not to touch)

You do not need to get on the roof. In fact, please don't. The IKO Certified crew at Pineapple will do a Complimentary Professional Photo Audit that produces a full photo set you can hand to your adjuster — at no cost, no obligation. Until then, here's what to capture from the ground and the street.

From the ground, photograph:

  • Soft metals first. Gutter downspouts, metal fascia, window screens, and A/C condenser fins take the clearest "hail signature" — and adjusters love them.
  • Shingle mat exposed at the eaves. Look up at the first row of shingles from underneath the eave. If you see black, shiny spots, granules have been knocked loose.
  • Skylights, vents, and pipe boots. Any cracked sealant, dented flashing, or bruised boot is a clean, photographic piece of evidence.
  • The yard. A close-up of hail stones on a ruler, in the same shot, is one of the strongest pieces of evidence in a claim.

What to skip:

  • Don't pressure-wash anything. Don't sweep granules off the driveway. Don't let anyone "test" the roof with a hammer.
  • Don't sign anything from a contractor who showed up uninvited. The "AOB" (Assignment of Benefits) trap is real, and it can hand control of your claim to someone else.
  • Don't pay a deposit. A legitimate local roofer does not need money to inspect your roof.

Hour 24–48: calling your insurance company

This is where most Frisco homeowners lose ground — not because the damage isn't real, but because the call goes sideways. Three rules keep you on the front foot.

1. Call, but don't commit

Open the claim, give the date of loss, give the address, and confirm the policy is in force. Don't accept a first settlement number on that call. Don't agree to a "cash-out for cosmetic damage" buyout. Don't accept an estimate you haven't seen. Just open the file.

2. Use the words "functional damage," not "cosmetic"

Texas insurance policies distinguish between cosmetic hail dings and damage that affects the life of the shingle. Granule loss, exposed mat, bruised shingles, and cracked flashing are all functional. If your adjuster calls it cosmetic and you're on a roof over 8 years old, push back — your policy almost certainly covers functional damage.

3. Request your own copy of the adjuster's report

You're entitled to it. Ask for the Xactimate estimate, the photo set, and the damage notes — in writing. If the scope feels light, that's the moment a Pineapple Photo Audit pays for itself ten times over.
